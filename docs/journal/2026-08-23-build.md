# 2026-08-23 — Call Her, from a Substack draft to 49 tests

Evidence, not instruction. What actually happened, including the parts that
are still unproven. The instruction extracted from this is the global
`sms-native-product` skill.

## What was asked

Aidan had drafted a Substack post: a dating app where you journal a first date
and it's shared only with your closest friends. His question was narrow and
already had an answer in it — *"what is the UX for this? … no app, no website.
You finish your first date and text us your journal entry … I think this might
be the ideal interface honestly."* Plus: he wants to publish it and use his 58
Substack readers as beta users.

So the real question was not "what should the UX be." It was **"is the
text-message interface actually buildable, and what breaks."**

## What I got wrong first

**I asserted from memory that Twilio can't do group MMS in the US.** I nearly
told him his ideal interface required a relay-hub workaround. Checked before
saying it: Twilio Conversations *does* do group texting on +1 long codes, up to
10 participants, agent included. Memory was stale. The check cost one search.

**I then built a real constraint into a false dilemma.** I told him a native
group thread and his 2-week lockout were mutually exclusive, because a real
group thread can't hold replies back. True as far as it went — and he dissolved
it in one line: *"maybe you text your journal, and our agent responds 'do you
want to open the group chat now? or wait 1 week…'"* The room doesn't exist yet
when the entry is posted, so there is nothing to hold back. The delay is in
**opening the door, not holding the mail.** His framing was strictly better
than mine and removed the need for an architecture I'd just argued for.

Then it moved once more, on my side: per-date rooms would have hit Twilio's
participant↔proxy uniqueness rule ([50425](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/50425)),
which has *no documented behaviour* for group-MMS participants (they carry an
Address and no ProxyAddress). So: **one standing room per creator**, and WAIT
becomes a delayed *post* into an existing room. Same mechanic, no undocumented
routing. Which landed back on his own original words — "why not make it a group
chat?", singular.

## What Aidan decided vs. what I decided

**His**, and not to be re-litigated:
- Text messages as the interface. No app, no website as the product.
- The NOW/WAIT choice, offered per entry by the agent after the entry lands.
- A two-sided web page: one side for the person dating, one for inviting a
  friend to do it.
- Free, public benefit.
- New public repos in his GitHub.

**Mine**, and reversible:
- One standing room per creator instead of per date (forced by 50425).
- The day-7 chase being the mechanism, not the journaling. He hasn't confirmed
  this framing; it's my read of his own stated goal ("incentivizing men to call
  the damn girl back").
- Refusing to build a decoy landing page to get past campaign vetting.
- Pure machine + injectable channel + simulator.
- `Sun Aug 30` over `Sunday`; names refused; the contact saved as "Call Her".

## The three external findings that shaped the build

All checked against primary sources, all load-bearing:

1. **A webpage cannot read an iPhone address book.** The Contact Picker API is
   Chrome-on-Android only; on iOS Safari it sits behind an off-by-default
   experimental flag. This killed his "sync your contacts" step outright. The
   replacement — forward a `join <code>` line — is better, because the buddy's
   inbound text is the consent record carriers require anyway.
2. **Campaign vetting names this product category by hand.** A linked site
   hosting "pornographic, escort, **dating**, or adult entertainment content"
   is rejected under [30953](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30953),
   **not eligible for resubmission**. And risk patterns follow the tax ID
   ([30898](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30898)), so this must not
   touch the Brick & Mortar EIN.
3. **Group MMS SMS participants have no proxy address**, so one Twilio number
   serves the whole system — but the uniqueness rule's behaviour for them is
   undocumented.

## The one thing I refused

I did not build a compliance-safe decoy page with the real product hidden
behind it, and said so plainly: a page that misrepresents the traffic *is* the
high-risk pattern that gets brands killed, so it would put every number he ever
registers at risk instead of one. `docs/COMPLIANCE.md` states the honest odds
instead — this page says "first date" and there is a real chance a reviewer
keyword-matches it and refuses permanently on the first try.

That refusal is what made the Telegram channel worth building the same day
rather than leaving it as a documented idea.

## Driving it found what tests didn't

`sim/drive.js` runs `lib/engine.js` — the production path, with an in-memory
store and a console channel. The first drive produced four real defects, none of
which any unit test on the pure machine would ever have found:

1. The room greeted itself. `add_to_room` created the conversation and posted
   "this is Aidan's circle" while the creator was its only member.
2. `Sunday` seven days out reads as the Sunday two days away.
3. The day-7 question said "about this one," pointing at a message seven days up
   a thread.
4. **A drained circle.** READY is earned once, but every member can reply STOP.
   A creator whose circle emptied would post an entry into a room with nobody in
   it, and bank it.

(4) is the one that mattered, and it paid for itself an hour later: when the
Telegram bot is kicked from a group, the same guard refuses the next entry
instead of reaching a `createRoom` that cannot exist on that channel.

One test failure was **my test being wrong, not the code** — I asserted a date
regex that didn't allow the comma in `Sun, Aug 30`. Worth recording because the
instinct on a red test is to change the code.

## The guard, run for real (later the same day)

Aidan pointed out there were two Anthropic keys on disk; I had only tried one,
and it was out of credit. The other one works — **check every candidate before
reporting a thing as blocked.**

Running it found two defects that no amount of reading the prompt would have:

1. **It invented a fact.** "Sarah Miller" became *"the girl from the app"* — and
   nothing in the entry mentions an app. Worse, it invented the opposite of the
   product's premise: he meets people in rooms, not on apps
   (`[[project_doors_meeting_people]]`). His friends would have read a
   fabrication as fact.
2. **Then it over-corrected.** Told to ground the handle in the entry, it
   deleted a real detail: "Rachel from the run club" became "her". The name had
   to go; the run club did not.

The final rule is three clauses — ground the handle in the text, replace the
name and not the phrase around it, and fall back to a plain pronoun rather than
reuse a detail the sentence already says. Six live tests pin all of it, opt-in
via `npm run test:live` so the default suite stays free.

Latency 2–5s per entry. Fine for a text reply.
