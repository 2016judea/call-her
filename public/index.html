Call Her

You said you'd call her.Did you?

Text it how the date went. It lands in a group chat with the two friends you'd have told anyway — and a week later it asks, in front of them, if you called.

Start on Telegram →

Free. No app to install, no account, nothing to log into. It runs inside a chat.

How to start

  1. Message the bot

    Open @callher_bot and send /start. It asks what your friends should see you as.

  2. Add it to the group chat you already have

    Don't make a new one. Open the group with the friends you'd text about this anyway, add @callher_bot as a member, and send /claim in it.

    That group is your circle. Nobody gets added by anyone but themselves, and nobody outside it ever sees a word.

  3. After a first date, text it

    A few sentences, however you'd write them. It asks one question back: NOW or WAIT.

  4. Then it holds you to it

    NOW puts the entry in the group straight away, and seven days later asks the group whether you called.

    WAIT holds it for a week so nobody can talk you out of a decision you haven't made yet — then posts the entry and what you actually did, together.

The rules it keeps

Her name never leaves your phone.
Every entry is read before it goes anywhere, and any name in it is swapped for a handle — "the architect", "the girl from the run club". She didn't sign up for this.
Nothing is public, ever.
There is no feed, no profile, no upvotes and no browsing. Entries go to one group chat that you assembled, and nowhere else.
Nobody joins without joining.
No contact list is read, no number is imported. Your circle is whoever is already in the group chat you added the bot to.
You get one update, and it asks for it.
What happened. Whether you called. That's the whole point — the journaling is just how you get there.
Leaving is one word.
Send STOP and you're out and forgotten. Or remove the bot from the group, and it stops.
