Call Her

You already text two friends after a date.Make it a group chat.

Journal it to the handful of people you let in. They talk it over — and two weeks later you tell them what you did. Which is usually the part that gets you to call her.

Start on Telegram →

Free. No app to install, no account, nothing to log into. It runs inside a chat.

How to start

  1. Message the bot

    Open @callher_bot and send /start. It asks what your friends should see you as.

  2. Add it to the group chat you already have

    Don't make a new one. Open the group with the friends you'd text about this anyway, add @callher_bot as a member, and send /claim in it.

    That group is your circle. Nobody gets added by anyone but themselves, and nobody outside it ever sees a word.

  3. After a first date, text it

    A few sentences, however you'd write them. It goes to the group either way — that's the whole point. The one question back is whether you want to hear from them yet: NOW or WAIT.

  4. They talk. Two weeks later, you talk

    NOW and their replies land as they come.

    WAIT posts the same entry with a line asking them to let you decide first. You're in that group chat, so nothing can technically hide their replies from you — but everyone can see the ask.

    Two weeks after the date it asks you, on your own, what happened. One update, and it's yours to give.

The rules it keeps

Her name never leaves your phone.
Every entry is read before it goes anywhere, and any name in it is swapped for a handle — "the one who ordered the whole fish", "the girl from the bookstore". She didn't sign up for this.
Nothing is public, ever.
There is no feed, no profile, no upvotes and no browsing. Entries go to one group chat that you assembled, and nowhere else.
Nobody joins without joining.
No contact list is read, no number is imported. Your circle is whoever is already in the group chat you added the bot to.
Your friends read it the day you write it.
Nothing is held back from the room. Journaling is good; journaling to the handful of people who'll be honest with you is better, and a group chat nobody can talk in isn't one.
One update, and it's yours to give.
Two weeks on it asks you what happened — privately, not the room. Long enough that you've already decided, so nobody talked you out of it. Then your words go in, and that entry is closed.
Leaving is one word.
Send STOP and you're out and forgotten. Or remove the bot from the group, and it stops.
